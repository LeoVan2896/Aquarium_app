# Soft water and patience pick your stock

A 20-gallon planted aquasoil tank at about 74°F suits **soft-water nano schoolers, small corydoras and low-light epiphytes**. It does not suit hard-water livebearers, snails, cold-water minnows or heat-loving cichlids. In Seriously Fish's per-species ranges, 74°F (23.3°C) sits inside the comfort band of nearly every beginner fish. The exceptions are the White Cloud Mountain minnow (max 72°F), Endler's livebearer (min 75°F) and, among intermediate fish, the German blue ram (81–86°F). Temperature is therefore rarely what limits this tank. Water chemistry is. Active substrates "lower KH" and "stabilize pH at low levels" ([Shrimply Explained](https://www.shrimplyexplained.com/tools/are-your-tank-parameters-good-for-neocaridina-shrimp/)), which favours neons, embers, harlequins, chili rasboras, pencilfish, kuhlis and pygmy or panda corys. The same chemistry works against guppies, platies, nerites and mystery snails, which all want pH of 7 or higher. The second constraint is maturity: the tank is still cycling. Otocinclus, shrimp, rams, Boraras, Pseudomugil, Iriatherina and Nannostomus are all flagged by their sources as never to be added to an immature tank. For plants, the safest picks with no CO₂ and unknown light wattage are the ones both Tropica and Flowgrow rate low-light: Anubias, Java fern, Java moss, dwarf sagittaria and Cryptocoryne wendtii. The step to "intermediate" plants is mainly a step up in CO₂ and light, not in temperature, because every shortlisted plant tolerates 74°F. Every value below carries its source. "not found" means no fetched source stated it, and source disagreements are recorded side by side rather than resolved.

**How to read the tables.** Fish values come primarily from Seriously Fish (SF), which gives metric units. °F, inch and dGH figures are arithmetic conversions of SF's °C, mm and ppm values (dGH ≈ ppm ÷ 17.85), not new data. SF states minimum tank size as a base footprint. SF itself equates 60×30 cm with about 54 L / 14 US gal ([SF platy](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/xiphophorus-maculatus/)) and 45×30 cm with about 41 L / 11 US gal ([SF zebra oto](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/otocinclus-cocama/)). Sizes are mostly standard length (SL), which excludes the tail. SF never gives KH for fish, so a fish KH field will be "not found" almost everywhere. The "Fit" column is this report's assessment for the user's tank: 20 gal, planted, aquasoil, ~74°F, still cycling. The assessment rests on the cited ranges, and **the tank's actual pH, GH, KH and dimensions (20 high vs 20 long) are unknown**.

## Seriously Fish ranges clear 74°F for 18 of 20 beginner fish

Most small community fish cluster around **70–79°F, pH 6–7.5 and a 60×30 cm (~14 gal) base**, so a 20-gallon tank has room for one school plus a bottom group. The fit verdicts turn on three things: pH (livebearers need 7.0+), footprint (rosy barb and bronze cory want a longer base than a typical 20), and temperature at the edges of a range.

## Intermediate fish fail on maturity, live food and male aggression, not hardiness

The intermediate tier is defined by why a species is harder, not by how fragile it is. Six recurring drivers appear: a mature, stable tank (rams, Boraras, Iriatherina, Pseudomugil, Nannostomus, Sewellia, otos); live or frozen food (pea puffer, scarlet badis, Iriatherina, Pseudomugil); specific soft water (rams, rummy-nose, crystal reds); male territoriality (Apistogramma, Dario, puffer, Gardner's killi); unusual equipment (Sewellia's high flow, crystal reds' RO water); and poor competitiveness that rules out a general community. These drivers map directly onto an `intermediate_reason` enum.

## Low-light epiphytes and crypts form the safe beginner plant core

Two catalog warnings follow from the plant notes. First, **terrestrial houseplants are routinely sold as aquatics** and die submerged. The documented examples are mondo grass, dracaena/"lucky bamboo", aluminum plant, Japanese rush (*Acorus*, not *Blyxa*), caladium, parlor palm, arrowhead vine, Chinese evergreen, club moss and peace lily, which is sometimes sold as "Brazilian sword" ([Microcosm Aquarium Explorer](https://en.microcosmaquariumexplorer.com/wiki/Worst_Aquarium_Plants); [Aquariadise](https://aquariadise.com/mondo-grass-aquarium/)). The app should key on scientific names, not trade names. Second, evidence on which fish eat plants is thin and anecdotal. The tough epiphytes are the most resistant, and Flowgrow even tags Anubias nana a "cichlid proof plant". Soft stems like anacharis get eaten ([Hepper](https://articles.hepper.com/best-aquarium-plants-for-goldfish)). For most plants, "eaten by" should stay "not found".

The failure modes are concrete and diagnosable. *C. parva* develops chlorosis and holes when iron or potassium runs low. *Myriophyllum* shoot tips go white without iron and micronutrients. *P. helferi* stretches and yellows under weak light ([Aquasabi](https://www.aquasabi.de/Cryptocoryne-parva_1); [Aquasabi](https://www.aquasabi.de/Myriophyllum-mattogrossense_1); [Aquasabi](https://www.aquasabi.de/Pogostemon-helferi_1)). Tissue-culture cups carry no nursery nutrients and "need the addition of liquid fertiliser… as well as CO2 injection from the start" ([Tropica 1-2-Grow!](https://tropica.com/ProductMedia/Artikler/641%20UK/Download.pdf)).

The upgrade path itself is quantified. Sources disagree on where "medium" light sits: 40–90 PAR for "Nature style" tanks per 2Hr Aquarist ([2Hr Aquarist](https://www.2hraquarist.com/en-au/blogs/light-3pillars/planted-tank-lighting-101)), 20–35 per Aquarium Co-Op ([ACO](https://aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/par-for-planted-tanks)), and 40–70 per Gensou ([Gensou](https://gensou.sg/how-to-balance-light-and-co2-planted-tank/)). The first two figures come from search summaries, not full fetches. All three agree that above roughly 40–50 PAR, CO₂ becomes the limit. Tom Barr puts the optimum at **20–30 ppm during the photoperiod** and says "95% of the time" failures trace to CO₂ being too low ([Barr Report](https://barrreport.com/articles/the-estimative-index-of-dosing-or-no-need-for-test-kits.38/)). On fertilizer, 2Hr Aquarist calls EI "too rich for many tanks" and ADA's lean regime "a little too lean (especially when the Aquasoil substrate wears out)". It recommends a middle ground of weekly K 10–18, NO₃ 5–12, PO₄ 2–8 ppm ([2Hr Aquarist](https://www.2hraquarist.com/blogs/fertilize-planted-tank/how-much-to-dose)). For a 10–20 gal EI tank, GWAPA gives about 1/8 tsp KNO₃, 1/32 tsp KH₂PO₄ and 2 ml traces, each three times a week, with a 50% weekly change ([GWAPA](https://gwapa.org/articles/dosing/)). Tropica's "Advanced" plants (HC 'Cuba', *Glossostigma*, *Rotala wallichii*, *R. macrandra* and others) are out of scope. Tropica itself suggests Monte Carlo as the "less demanding" alternative to HC ([Tropica](https://tropica.com/en/plants/plantdetails/Micranthemumcallitrichoides'Cuba'(048BTC)/4478)).

## Your cycling tank should stock soft-water nanos, in a strict order

Put the constraints together and the tank has a clear identity: a **soft, slightly acidic, low-tech nano community**. Aquasoil pushes chemistry toward the ranges of neons, embers, harlequins, chili rasboras, pencilfish, Pseudomugil, pygmy and panda corys and an Apistogramma pair. At 74°F, panda corys sit inside a band that SF says shortens lifespan above 77°F. CRS is near its ceiling. The heat-lovers (German blue ram, Endler's) and the cold-lovers (White Cloud minnow) fall out. Three caveats need tests rather than assumptions. Neocaridina wants pH ≥6.5 and GH ≥6, and ShrimpKeepers warns active soil "can lower pH too much" for it ([ShrimpKeepers](https://www.shrimpkeepers.com/guides/neocaridina/)). Celestial pearl danios want at least 5 dGH. Kuhlis want sand.

Order matters more than species choice while the tank cycles. Nothing should go in before the cycle completes. CRS keepers should expect new active soil to leach ammonia and allow **4–6 weeks** of cycling ([Fast Aquatics](https://fastaquatics.com/care/crystal-red-shrimp-breeding/)). Cherry shrimp belong in "a mature tank that has been running for many months" ([ACO](https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/cherry-shrimp-care)). Otos "should never be added to an immature aquarium" because they "may well starve" ([SF](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/otocinclus-cocama/)). Rams, Boraras, Iriatherina, Pseudomugil and Nannostomus carry the same SF warning. A sensible sequence is therefore: hardy schoolers first (harlequin, ember, cherry barb), then corys and a bristlenose, then the maturity-dependent species. That sequence is this report's synthesis of the per-species flags, not a sourced protocol.

Several compatibility conflicts belong in the app's checker. SF names gouramis among the fish that eat pygmy corys, and sparkling gouramis reportedly prey on small shrimp. Pea puffers nip slow or long-finned fish, which rules out guppies, bettas and threadfin males as tankmates. CPDs hybridise with *C. erythromicron*. Temperature ranges must be compared alongside temperament: a German blue ram at 81–86°F shares no overlap with CRS or Sewellia at 68–75°F ([SF](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/mikrogeophagus-ramirezi/)).

The avoid list is short and well documented. The **common pleco** exceeds 20 in yet is bought "for tanks as small as 40 l/10 gal" ([Practical Fishkeeping](https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/the-common-plec-a-big-problem/)). The **iridescent shark** "easily tops 90 cm" and lives 20+ years ([SF](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/pangasianodon-hypophthalmus/)). The **clown loach** reaches 8–12 in, needs a 180×60 cm base for a group, and must never go into immature set-ups ([SF](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/chromobotia-macracanthus/)). The **bala shark** is "among those species most frequently returned having outgrown small aquaria" ([SF](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/balantiocheilos-melanopterus/)). Goldfish need about 30 gallons each and angelfish 55+ gallons ([ACO](https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/beginner-fish)). ACO's beginner list includes those last two because they are hardy, not because they suit small tanks.

## Source disagreements the catalog should store, not resolve

The disagreements follow patterns, which tells the schema what to do with them. **For fish, the gap is usually range breadth versus specificity.** ACO gives tetras a genus-level pH of 7.0–7.8 ([ACO](https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/beginner-fish)), which contradicts SF's species-level 4.0–7.5 for neons and 5.0–7.0 for embers. The species-level SF figure is the better default. Group-size disagreements (kuhli 5–6 vs 3–6, corys 4–6 vs 3–6) should default to SF's larger minimum. Two disputes are genuine, not semantic. Betta community suitability: SF says alone, ACO says OK in 10+ gal. Pea puffer teeth: SF says feed snails to wear the teeth down, ACO says pea puffers "don't have this problem". Both should be shown, not averaged.

**For shrimp, the gap is "comfortable" versus "survivable".** Cherry shrimp temperature runs from 57–86°F to 65–80°F depending on which ACO or ShrimpKeepers page is read ([ShrimpKeepers](https://www.shrimpkeepers.com/species/cherry-shrimp/); [ACO](https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/breeding-red-cherry-shrimp)). That argues for separate `optimal` and `tolerated` range fields.

**For plants, the gap is mostly light and CO₂ categories, plus height semantics.** Tropica rates Egeria, Bacopa, Amazon sword and Ludwigia 'Super Red' low-light. Flowgrow or Aquasabi rate them medium-to-high. Tropica's height is "after two months". Aquasabi's is a "recommended care height". Flowgrow's is sometimes a maximum. These are three different fields, not one. Some source data is internally unreliable. The Aquasabi/Flowgrow table gives AR 'Mini' a 39–86°F tolerance while Aquasabi's own text says 72–82°F, and Tropica's Ammannia page says "Medium" light in its table and "high light demands" in its text. Where a source contradicts itself, the narrower range is the safer catalog default.

The remaining gaps are known and finite. Fish KH, swim level, plant-eating and digging are mostly not stated anywhere. Temperature and pH are missing for *C. wendtii*, *Marsilea minuta*, *Gratiola*, Rotala 'Blood Red', AR 'Rosanervig', *Ammannia*, *P. deccanensis* and *Juncus*. Group size is missing for guppies and black skirt tetras, and tank size for zebra danios and Gardner's killi. FishBase was never consulted, so scientific names and maximum sizes rest on SF alone. *Hygrophila polysperma*'s US legal status needs verifying before the app recommends it.

## Conclusion

The research changes the right question for this tank from "which fish are hardy?" to "which fish match the chemistry aquasoil creates, and when?" Hardiness ratings from ACO and SF agree broadly. What separates good fits from bad in this specific tank is pH direction, a narrow temperature band, and tank maturity. None of those appear in a generic "beginner fish" list. That argues for a catalog built around compatibility filters rather than difficulty tiers: a species should be matched against the tank's measured pH, GH, temperature, footprint and age, and also against its tankmates' ranges. The most valuable fields are therefore the unglamorous ones: `needs_mature_tank`, `stock_quality_warning`, `optimal` vs `tolerated` ranges, and a per-value `source` tag.

Two implications follow for the user's own tank. First, testing pH, GH and KH once cycling ends is the single highest-value action. That one result decides whether cherry shrimp, CPDs, livebearers or snails are viable, or whether the tank should lean fully into soft water with Boraras, pencilfish and possibly crystal reds. Second, aquasoil's buffering has a stated 12–18-month lifespan. The chemistry that makes soft-water species ideal now will drift, so the app should prompt periodic re-testing rather than treat substrate as a fixed property.
